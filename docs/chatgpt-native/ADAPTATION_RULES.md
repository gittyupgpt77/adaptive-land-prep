# Adaptive Land Prep — adaptation and safety rules

## Role of this file
These are product guardrails derived from the archived PWA. They are not diagnostic thresholds and do not certify injury-free readiness. ChatGPT may use broader reasoning, but it must not silently violate these rules or invent a harder dose.

## Daily inputs
Use the best available source for:
- sleep duration and sleep quality
- sleep HRV and personal HRV baseline
- resting heart rate and personal baseline
- subjective fatigue
- grip strength when available, compared with same-device/same-unit baseline
- perceived performance relative to normal
- current pain 0–10
- focal bone pain yes/no
- altered gait yes/no
- previous session RPE and post-session pain
- confirmed recent calorie/carbohydrate intake
- current planned training load

## Systemic recovery domains
Treat these as explicit guardrails:
- Sleep RED: <6 h or sleep quality ≤2/5.
- Sleep YELLOW: <7 h or sleep quality =3/5.
- Fatigue RED: ≥4/5.
- Fatigue YELLOW: 3/5.
- Autonomic RED: HRV <85% of baseline OR RHR > baseline +8 bpm.
- Autonomic YELLOW: HRV <92% of baseline OR RHR > baseline +5 bpm.
- Neuromuscular YELLOW: grip <90% of baseline.
- Prior-session RPE ≥9/10 is contextual YELLOW.
- Phase 1 (Weeks 1–16): one “performance not normal” report is context only; repeated poor performance within ~3 calendar days becomes YELLOW.
- After Week 16: one poor-performance report is YELLOW; persistence can support RED.

Do not promote one noisy marker directly to a hard stop. Strong systemic intervention requires either corroboration across domains or persistence of the same abnormal domain over recent check-ins.

Systemic overall:
- RED if at least two domains are RED, or a RED domain persists across recent check-ins.
- YELLOW if one RED exists, at least two YELLOW domains exist, or a YELLOW domain persists.
- Otherwise GREEN.

## Mechanical domain
Mechanical status overrides favorable recovery:
- RED for focal bone pain, altered gait, current pain ≥5/10, or previous-session pain ≥5/10.
- YELLOW for current pain 3–4/10 or previous-session pain 3–4/10.
- GREEN otherwise.

A mechanical RED means:
- no running
- no weighted-pack walking
- easy row or walk only if pain-free
- strength only if non-aggravating
- no hard training

## Fueling domain
Only confirmed intake can trigger under-fueling logic. Missing/incomplete logging is unknown, not under-eating.
- If previous confirmed intake <65% of target and planned load is high (≥6/10): RED.
- If previous confirmed intake <80% of target and planned load is moderate or higher (≥4/10): YELLOW.
- If no planned-load value exists, confirmed intake <70% of target may justify YELLOW context, not a RED conclusion.

## Overall daily decision
- Mechanical RED always produces a mechanical recovery override.
- Otherwise any systemic RED produces a recovery-priority day.
- YELLOW recovery/mechanical state or RED fueling produces a modified day.
- GREEN supports the planned curriculum; it does not authorize exceeding it.

### RED systemic/recovery day
- run: row or walk only
- ruck: no loaded walking
- row: recovery row
- strength: reduce about 50%
- intensity: no hard training

### YELLOW day
- run: reduce about 25–40%, keep easy
- ruck: reduce distance and/or load
- row: prefer easy rowing
- strength: reduce about 30%
- intensity: no hard intervals
- any quality/threshold segment becomes easy aerobic work

### GREEN day
Follow the earned curriculum, current weekly ceilings and calibrated intensity.

## Dose reductions
When reducing a session, translate the reduction into concrete sets/minutes rather than making the athlete do arithmetic. Do not multiply or reduce weekly ceilings as though they were today's dose.

## Running/ruck progression
- Weekly mileage ceilings are limits, never quotas.
- Do not “catch up” missed mileage.
- Do not increase pack load merely to compensate for reduced distance.
- Preserve normal gait and stop impact/loaded walking for focal pain or gait alteration.
- Later high-volume ceilings are aspirational maxima and must be earned through phase criteria and prior tolerated work.

## Intensity calibration
For quality run/row work, use a current established pace, HR, power or lactate calibration. If unavailable or stale, do not guess. Substitute easy Concept2 work within the week's allowed row volume.

## Fat-loss / nutrition change control
The user's athlete-directed fat-loss/calorie policy is change-controlled.
- Do not silently replace the user's chosen fat-loss priority with a maintenance/performance objective.
- If evidence or a safety concern conflicts with the current policy, explain the conflict and discuss it before changing the encoded prescription.
- Agreement alone is not proof of physiological safety.
- Do not derive a “safe deficit” directly from the historical fat-energy-transfer equation.
- Recalculate body-composition-dependent references when new paired measurements exist; do not hold an old body-fat percentage constant.
- During the Foundation phase, fat loss is prioritized while lean-tissue retention and basic health/recovery act as constraints; performance optimization is secondary.
- Preserve the established 14-day trend guardrail rather than reacting to one weigh-in.

## Nutrition adaptation
- Incomplete intake logging is unknown.
- Confirmed substantial under-fueling can modify training and should prompt carbohydrate/energy review.
- A red fueling signal calls for added energy/carbohydrate and review of the deficit.
- A yellow fueling signal emphasizes carbohydrate placement around training without automatically rewriting the calorie policy.
- Micronutrient adequacy is a separate periodic audit, not inferred from calories/macros.
